Before you measure 7 tips for a true reading
  1. 30 minutes before: no coffee, tea, energy drinks, smoking, alcohol or exercise.
  2. Empty your bladder first. A full bladder raises the reading.
  3. Sit and rest 5 minutes in a quiet place. Back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs uncrossed.
  4. Arm straight and relaxed on a table, palm up, with the cuff at heart level.
  5. Cuff on bare skin, its lower edge 2–3 cm above the elbow crease. Use a cuff that fits your arm.
  6. Stay still and calm. Don't talk, move or use your phone while it measures.
  7. Take 2–3 readings, 1 minute apart, at the same times each day: morning before medicine and breakfast, and evening before dinner.

What to do: first aid

Emergency in Denmark: 112
Very high: above 180/120

Call 112 now if there is any of these

  • Chest pain, pressure or tightness
  • Shortness of breath
  • Face drooping, or weakness or numbness in an arm or leg, especially on one side
  • Slurred speech, trouble speaking or understanding, or sudden confusion
  • Sudden severe headache, or sudden loss of vision
  • Severe back or belly pain
  • Fainting or a seizure
Call 112If the button does nothing, dial 112 on your phone.

If you have none of these signs

  1. Sit down with your back supported and both feet on the floor.
  2. Rest for 5 minutes. Breathe slowly: in for 4 seconds, out for 6 seconds.
  3. Measure again on the same arm.
  4. If it is still above 180/120, call your doctor now. When your doctor is closed, call the after-hours doctor service (1813 in the Capital Region, lægevagten in other regions). If you can't reach anyone, call 112.
  5. Take your usual blood pressure medicine only if a dose is due, exactly as prescribed. Do not take extra doses or anyone else's medicine.

While you wait for help

  • Stay sitting upright and keep still. Do not drive yourself.
  • Unlock the front door and keep your phone with you.
  • Do not eat, smoke, or drink coffee or alcohol.
  • Take aspirin only if the 112 dispatcher tells you to, for example for chest pain that may be a heart attack. Never take it for possible stroke signs unless told to.
  • Show the ambulance crew your readings and medicine list below.

Step by step for a helper

  1. If any warning sign above is present, call 112. Put the phone on speaker and follow the dispatcher.
  2. Help the person sit comfortably, upright or half sitting. Loosen tight clothing. Stay with them and keep them calm.
  3. Check for stroke with FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time. Note the time the symptoms started and tell the crew.
  4. Do not give food, drink or medicine unless the dispatcher says so.
  5. If they become unresponsive but breathe normally, roll them onto their side in the recovery position. Keep checking their breathing.
  6. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR and follow the dispatcher's instructions: push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, 5–6 cm deep, 100–120 times a minute. Ask someone to fetch a defibrillator (hjertestarter). The dispatcher can tell you where the nearest one is.
  7. Give the ambulance crew the readings and medicine list from this log.
High: 140/90 or above, with no warning signs

If you are pregnant, a reading of 160/110 or above counts as very high: follow the section above. Otherwise, there is no safe way to bring blood pressure down quickly at home, and a fast drop can itself be harmful. The aim is to rest, check again and involve the doctor.

  1. Sit quietly for 5 minutes, then take two readings one minute apart.
  2. For the rest of the day, avoid coffee, smoking, alcohol, salty food and hard exercise.
  3. Keep taking your regular medicines as prescribed. Don't skip doses.
  4. If readings stay at 140/90 or above over several days, book an appointment with your doctor.
  5. If any warning sign from the very high section appears, call 112.
  1. Help them sit and rest quietly for 5 minutes, then measure again.
  2. Ask about chest pain, breathing, headache, vision, weakness and speech. Any of these means calling 112.
  3. Offer water, not coffee or alcohol.
  4. Encourage them to contact their doctor if readings stay high.

For an accurate reading

  • No coffee, smoking or exercise for 30 minutes before. Empty your bladder first.
  • Sit with your back supported, feet flat and your arm resting at heart height.
  • Don't talk while measuring. Use the same arm each time.
Low: below 90/60, dizziness or fainting

Call 112 if there is any of these

  • Fainted and does not wake up within a minute
  • Chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or shortness of breath
  • Confusion, or pale, cold and clammy skin with a fast weak pulse
  • Heavy bleeding, black stools or vomiting blood
  • A low reading after a fall or injury
Call 112If the button does nothing, dial 112 on your phone.
  1. Lie down straight away and raise your legs about 30 cm on a chair or cushions. If you can't lie down, sit and put your head between your knees.
  2. Stay down until the dizziness passes. Then sit for a minute before standing up slowly.
  3. When you feel fully alert, drink one or two glasses of water.
  4. If you have diabetes, check your blood sugar if you can. If it is low, or you feel shaky and sweaty, take fast sugar such as juice or glucose tablets.
  5. Measure again after 10 to 15 minutes.
  6. Tell your doctor if low readings with dizziness happen again, especially after starting a new medicine.
  1. Help them lie down and raise their legs about 30 cm. Loosen tight clothing and let in fresh air.
  2. If they faint, check breathing. If they are breathing, roll them onto their side in the recovery position and call 112 if they don't wake within a minute.
  3. If they are not breathing normally, call 112, start CPR and send for a defibrillator.
  4. Give nothing to eat or drink until they are fully awake and can swallow.
  5. Don't let them stand up quickly. Stay with them. If they fell and may have hurt their head, neck or back, don't move them unless you have to.

Everyday habits

Less salt

Aim for under 5 g a day, about one teaspoon. Most salt hides in bread, cheese, cold cuts and ready meals, so check labels.

WHO on salt

Eat for your heart

Fill half the plate with vegetables. Choose whole grains, fish, beans, nuts and low-fat dairy. This is the DASH eating style, shown to lower blood pressure.

About the DASH diet

Alcohol

The Danish Health Authority advises at most 10 units a week and no more than 4 on any one day. Less is better for blood pressure.

Sundhedsstyrelsen on alcohol

Stop smoking

Each cigarette raises blood pressure for a while and damages blood vessels. Free help in Denmark: Stoplinien, 80 31 31 31.

stoplinien.dk

Sleep

Most adults need 7 to 9 hours. Loud snoring with pauses in breathing can be sleep apnoea, which raises blood pressure. Mention it to your doctor.

Healthy weight

If you are overweight, losing even a few kilos lowers blood pressure, roughly 1 mmHg for each kilo lost.

Calm the mind

Stress raises blood pressure. A few minutes of slow breathing, a walk outside or time with friends helps.

Measure at home

Measure at the same times each day, for example morning and evening, and bring the export to your doctor.

How to do it

    Help

    Getting started

    Open Heartbeat and pick your name on the start screen, or tap Create profile. Fill in name and birth date. Everything else is optional but makes the advice more personal. Use Switch person to change between family members.

    Put Heartbeat on your iPhone home screen

    Open the link in Safari, tap the Share button (square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open the browser menu and choose Add to Home screen.

    How to measure blood pressure correctly
    • No coffee, smoking or exercise for 30 minutes before. Empty your bladder.
    • Sit for 5 minutes with your back supported and feet flat on the floor.
    • Rest your arm on a table with the cuff at heart height, on bare skin.
    • Don't talk. Take two readings one minute apart and log both.
    • Measure at the same times each day, for example morning and evening.
    Adding a reading

    On the Add tab, type the top number (systolic) and the bottom number (diastolic). Pulse and a note are optional. Date and time are set to now. Tap the time line to change them. Tap Save reading and the result appears at once with its colour.

    What the colours mean
    • Normal: below 120/80.
    • Elevated: top 120–129, bottom below 80.
    • High, stage 1: top 130–139 or bottom 80–89.
    • High, stage 2: 140/90 or above.
    • Hypertensive crisis: above 180/120, or 160/110 or above in pregnancy. Follow the first aid steps.
    • Low: below 90/60. Only a concern if you feel dizzy or faint.
    • Children under 13 are not colour-coded, because their normal range depends on age, sex and height.

    Based on the AHA/ACC 2025 blood pressure guideline.

    Your doctor's target

    Your doctor may give you a personal target, for example 130/80. Add it under Profile then Edit details. Readings above it are marked "above target".

    Calling for help
    • 112: emergencies, for example chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs, or someone who has fainted and doesn't wake.
    • 1813: medical advice when your own doctor is closed, after 16:00 on weekdays and at weekends (Capital Region).
    • Your country: the app picks the emergency number from your phone's region setting. If it is wrong, choose your country under the call buttons. Numbers come from Wikipedia's list of emergency numbers (CC BY-SA 4.0). Check your local number before you need it.
    • Doctor: appears when you add your doctor's phone number to your profile.

    Tap a button to open your phone's dialer. If nothing happens, dial the number shown.

    First aid

    The First aid tab has steps for very high, high and low blood pressure. Switch between It's me and I'm helping someone. Your medicine list and last reading are shown so you can show them to the ambulance crew.

    Exercise and activity

    The Move tab shows exercises that help blood pressure and the heart, each with an illustration, steps and a guided timer. When you finish, tap Log minutes, or use Log activity for a walk or a bike ride. The ring shows your active minutes this week. Adults should aim for 150 minutes a week. Children aim for 60 minutes a day.

    Family, children and guardians

    Each family member signs in with their own Claude account and has their own private profile. Anyone under 18 is saved as a child. Children's records are shared with everyone you share Heartbeat with, so both parents or guardians can follow them. To share, use the Share menu and give the person Contributor access.

    Importing and exporting readings

    Under Profile, open Import past readings and paste one reading per line, for example 20.11.2026 08:15 135/85 72, or choose a .csv or .txt file. Export readings downloads a CSV file you can open in Excel or give to your doctor.

    Privacy and your data

    Your data stays in the app. In the standalone app it is saved only on your device. Inside Claude, adult profiles and readings are saved privately to your Claude account. Children's records are visible to everyone you share Heartbeat with. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else: no tracking, no ads. See Terms of use, disclaimer and privacy below for the details and your rights.

    Editing or removing a person

    Go to Profile, tap Edit details, change what you need and save. Remove person deletes the profile and all of its readings. It asks you to confirm first.

    Terms of use, disclaimer and privacy

    1. What Heartbeat is

    Heartbeat is a general wellness tool. It helps you and your family record blood pressure and pulse readings, see them over time, and find general information about measuring, healthy habits, exercise and first aid. The colour bands, first aid steps and exercises are general information based on published guidelines (American Heart Association 2025, European first aid guidelines 2025 and others named in the app).

    2. Not medical advice

    Heartbeat does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease, and it does not replace a doctor, nurse or other health professional. It gives no personal medical advice and does not recommend, change or stop any medicine. Never ignore professional advice, or delay seeking it, because of something in the app. Always follow your own doctor's advice, and the emergency dispatcher's instructions in an emergency.

    3. Emergencies

    If you think you or someone else is having a medical emergency, call your local emergency number straight away. Emergency numbers in the app come from public lists and may be out of date or wrong for where you are. Learn your local number before you need it. A call button may not work on every device, so be ready to dial the number yourself.

    4. Accuracy of readings

    The app only shows the numbers you type in. Their accuracy depends on your blood pressure monitor and on how you measure. Use a validated monitor and follow the "Before you measure" tips. Errors in typing, in your monitor, or in the app are possible.

    5. Children

    Profiles for people under 18 are created and managed by a parent or guardian, who is responsible for the data they enter. Readings for children under 13 are not colour-coded, because normal ranges depend on age, sex and height. Ask the child's doctor.

    6. Your data and privacy

    • What is stored: the profile details you enter (name, birth date, and optionally sex, height, weight, conditions, medicines, your doctor's target and phone number), your readings and notes, your activity log, and your country choice. Health data is a special category of personal data under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, Article 9). You choose to enter it, and you can delete it at any time.
    • Where it is stored: in the standalone app, only on your own device, in this browser's storage. Inside Claude, in your own Claude account's private storage for this app. Records of children are visible to the family members you share the app with.
    • What we do not do: the app does not send your data to us or to anyone else. There is no tracking, no analytics, no advertising, and your data is never sold or used outside the app.
    • Your rights: you can see all your data in the app, export it under Profile then Export, correct it with Edit details, and delete it with Remove person, which also deletes that person's readings. You can also complain to your data protection authority (in Denmark, Datatilsynet).
    • Keeping it safe: each person can set a PIN in Edit details. Heartbeat stores only a scrambled copy (a salted PBKDF2 hash), never the PIN itself, and locks again after 2 minutes away. The PIN stops others in your family from opening a profile in the app. It does not encrypt the stored data, so anyone who can unlock your phone or computer and knows how to read browser storage could still see it. Use a screen lock, and don't use Heartbeat on a shared or public device.
